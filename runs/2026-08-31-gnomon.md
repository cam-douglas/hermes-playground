Facts from the issue only:

- In `~/.claude/projects/<project>/`, a large batch of session transcripts share one identical filesystem mtime — the same single second — while their contents show no activity anywhere near that time. mtime no longer dates a session.
- Measured 2026-08-31: 1,206 top-level session `.jsonl` files across all project dirs; 1,090 distinct mtime epochs.
- Largest cluster: 114 files all at epoch `1787422837` (2026-08-22T21:20:37 local), all within a single project dir.
- For those 114, mtime minus the last timestamped record inside the file: min 3 days, median 17 days, max 47 days — 93 of 114 are stamped more than 7 days after their last real event.
- Tail record type of those files: `last-prompt` ×76, `mode` ×21, `attachment` ×10, `queue-operation` ×4, `user` ×2, `assistant` ×1.
- `last-prompt` and `mode` records carry **no `timestamp` field**. The write leaves no trace in content — only mtime moves.
- Two examples: a session whose last real event is 2026-07-22T14:00:18Z, and one whose last real event is 2026-08-04T10:19:24Z, both end with an untimed `last-prompt` record and both carry the 2026-08-22 mtime.
- The author has not identified what triggers the write and is deliberately not asserting a cause. Observable facts only: (1) many long-closed transcripts were appended to in one bulk operation, (2) the appended records are timestamp-free metadata types, (3) mtime moved and content did not.
- Either of these would fix it: writes to an already-closed transcript preserve its mtime, or any appended record carries a `timestamp` so the write is auditable from content. Currently neither holds.
- Why it matters: `ls -lt ~/.claude/projects/<project>/` reports a burst of 114 sessions on a day when no work happened; tooling that selects "recent sessions" by mtime silently operates on the wrong set; a retention policy keyed on mtime will preserve stale sessions and can age out recent ones. Silent-wrong-output.
- Regression: unknown — no known-good version to compare against.
